Why the score keeps hitting the ceiling

confluence_score=1.00 shows up in the rule-based fallback evidence because the score isn't measured against a fixed maximum — it's measured against itself. Any set of detectors that all agree pins the ratio at exactly 1.0, whether it's one weak signal or ten extreme ones. Here's the arithmetic, and where the confidence label rides along.

src/signals_app/scoring/confluence.pysrc/signals_app/synthesis/mtf_llm.py

1. The formula that's too easy to max out

Every firing detector casts a signed vote. The net score is the imbalance between bull and bear votes, divided by the total weight that actually fired — not by any fixed universe-wide ceiling.

ConfluenceRanker.rank_signals()
raw_score = (weighted_bull − weighted_bear) / max_weight
where max_weight = weighted_bull + weighted_bear  (the achieved total, not a cap)

When every firing detector votes the same direction, weighted_bear = 0, so the formula collapses to weighted_bull / weighted_bull = 1.0 — exactly, every time, regardless of how many detectors fired or how strong they were. The denominator was never an independent ceiling; it's built from the same votes as the numerator.

2. Unanimity beats conviction

Two tickers, same detector count, very different underlying conviction — the metric can't tell them apart.

One weak, unopposed signal
1.00
1 detector fires BULLISH (vote 1.0), nothing disagrees.
bull=1.0 / max=1.0
One extreme signal, one dissent
0.50
1 detector fires EXTREME_BULLISH (vote 3.0), 1 fires BEARISH (vote −1.0).
(3.0 − 1.0) / 4.0

The far stronger, better-evidenced ticker on the right scores lower than the flimsiest possible unanimous case on the left — because the score rewards agreement, not magnitude or sample size.

⚠ likely source of the "1.00" you're seeing

It's confluence_score, printed verbatim into the fallback evidence summary:

"Rule-based fallback: confluence_score={score:.2f}, change_pct=…"

That line comes from _fallback_signal() in mtf_llm.py — it fires whenever the LLM is disabled or fails, and just formats whatever ConfluenceResult.score handed it. A second, unrelated field also reads "1.0" in the same payload: the fallback's single evidence item always sets weight: 1.0, because a one-item evidence list trivially satisfies the "weights sum to 1.0" schema rule — that's not a confidence value at all, just the full weight of the only evidence item present.

5. What "too easy" means concretely

The gate that's supposed to filter for conviction only checks direction and count — never how close the vote actually was.

01

The denominator has no independent ceiling

max_weight is defined as the sum of whatever fired — it grows and shrinks with the numerator instead of being fixed relative to the full detector roster (18 detectors, up to 166 possible firings). A truly comprehensive, unanimous reading and a single-detector unanimous reading are mathematically identical.

02

confidence_label ignores sample size entirely

The HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW cut only looks at abs(score). Nothing in that branch consults bull_count / bear_count / total_signals, so a 1-signal read and a 6-signal read can both claim HIGH.

03

Action and confidence disagree on what "enough" means

action already has a signal-count floor (CONFLUENCE_BUY_MIN_SIGNALS = 3) that confidence_label doesn't share — which is how row 1 in §3 above ends up HOLD at HIGH confidence, a combination that reads as contradictory wherever it's surfaced.
